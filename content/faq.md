## Why doesn’t the statement encourage departments to clearly state in their hiring calls how the use of AI will be judged?

While this was not directly addressed at the workshop and did not make it into the statement as circulated, this is clearly a need, as confirmed by numerous conversations we have since had with junior researchers. We encourage hiring committees to include a statement of values with hiring calls, clarifying their broad expectations and evaluation criteria around AI use. If it is difficult to include such language due to institutional constraints, faculty in a department may consider doing this as individuals.

## Shouldn’t something like this be left to each department to decide?

Ultimately, it will be up to individual departments to decide and publicize the details of their hiring criteria.  The challenge is that this takes time and departments are incentivized to do this only at the moment when they announce job opportunities.  At the same time, young researchers and their mentors are seeking directional guidance now.  This signing initiative is meant to both encourage departments to take up this issue as well as signal to the young people the community’s jointly held values: quality over quantity and depth of understanding.


## Up to three is too rigid, or not the right number.

Three was actually chosen with care, as it is expected that the candidate's talk and the letters will cover the work in detail. If a candidate is covering more work in their talk, it is most likely that they aren't addressing the details with enough care.

## But what about the candidate who has a body of consistent work that consists of more than three papers?

It would be perfectly fine for that candidate to make the body of work clear while selecting the three works that contained the most significant ideas/techniques.

## I don't want to publicly commit to anything.

That's fine, but recognize that without a signal like this, young people are left without direction. In addition, the arms race of AI-assisted rushed results will continue to overwhelm the community systems in charge of trying to curate the most significant, interesting, and well-done results. Said another way: is the alternative, i.e. doing nothing, better?

## This won't age well. In a few years, we'll be in a whole different world.

This is meant to be a short-term action. As time goes on, the community may adjust to something else.
