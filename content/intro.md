In early September 2026, the Simons Center for Theoretical Computer Science held a working group called [*AI and TCS: the next six months*](https://simons.berkeley.edu/news-publications-videos/ai-tcs-working-group). The goal of the workshop was to recommend actions for the community to take in the next six months to help the field adjust to the new reality. The [full report](https://simons.berkeley.edu/AITCSreport?inline=) is available online.

One of the recommended actions (Action 7) concerned practices around hiring in the post-AI era. The two main challenges identified are that:

1. it has become less clear which traits to look for, and
2. their identification based on accomplishments may become harder in the presence of AI.

These challenges obfuscate what early career researchers view as success and how their mentors should guide them. Without a conscious change to our culture and incentive structures, we may find the junior community engaged in an "arms race" in the number of research publications at the expense of quality of understanding and exposition.

We believe that one step in the right direction is to gather consensus from the senior members of our community around norms for hiring and evaluation. In an effort to signal to the young people in the community the values of quality over quantity and depth of understanding, we are seeking signatures from tenured and tenure-track faculty in support of the statement on the right.
