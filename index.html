A Statement on Hiring in Theoretical Computer Science

Supporting quality over quantity and depth of understanding in the post-AI era.

Introduction Statement Sign FAQ Signatories Addendum

In early September 2026, the Simons Center for Theoretical Computer Science held a working group called AI and TCS: the next six months. The goal of the workshop was to recommend actions for the community to take in the next six months to help the field adjust to the new reality. The full report is available online.

One of the recommended actions (Action 7) concerned practices around hiring in the post-AI era. The two main challenges identified are that:

  1. it has become less clear which traits to look for, and
  2. their identification based on accomplishments may become harder in the presence of AI.

These challenges obfuscate what early career researchers view as success and how their mentors should guide them. Without a conscious change to our culture and incentive structures, we may find the junior community engaged in an “arms race” in the number of research publications at the expense of quality of understanding and exposition.

We believe that one step in the right direction is to gather consensus from the senior members of our community around norms for hiring and evaluation. In an effort to signal to the young people in the community the values of quality over quantity and depth of understanding, we are seeking signatures from tenured and tenure-track faculty in support of the following statement.

Statement

In the evaluation of future tenure-track hires in my field, I support the following:1

  1. Emphasize quality over quantity. In the evaluations of candidates, consider at most three works from July 2026 onwards (of the candidates’ choosing), in addition to any number of prior works before July 2026.
  2. Actively assess thorough understanding. Evaluate the thoroughness of the candidates’ understanding of the aforementioned works more than was done normally until now. This evaluation could take the form of a “chalk talk,” more mathematically substantial interview slots, and/or other methods of the department’s choosing.

1 In a manner consistent with university guidelines.

Sign the statement

If you are a tenured or tenure-track faculty member who works on theoretical computer science and you agree, please sign.

FAQ

Up to three is too rigid, or not the right number.
Three was actually chosen with care, as it is expected that the candidate’s talk and the letters will cover the work in detail. If a candidate is covering more work in their talk, it is most likely that they aren’t addressing the details with enough care.
But what about the candidate who has a body of consistent work that consists of more than three papers?
It would be perfectly fine for that candidate to make the body of work clear while selecting the three works that contained the most significant ideas/techniques.
I don’t want to publicly commit to anything.
That’s fine, but recognize that without a signal like this, young people are left without direction. In addition, the arms race of AI-assisted rushed results will continue to overwhelm the community systems in charge of trying to curate the most significant, interesting, and well-done results. Said another way: is the alternative, i.e. doing nothing, better?
This won’t age well. In a few years, we’ll be in a whole different world.
This is meant to be a short-term action. As time goes on, the community may adjust to something else.
I don’t want to be bound by this. I may want to change my mind.
You can. All you are saying is that you support this statement now. If you want to later remove your name, you can.

Addendum

The following text was added by the organizers in early November, informed by conversations with junior researchers. It was not discussed in the Simons Institute working group in September 2026, and is not part of the petition above.

There is a wide range of views in the mathematical community on the use of AI for research. In the absence of explicit messaging from departments, job applicants do not know whether disclosing AI use in an application is expected and whether AI use will be penalized, rewarded, or neither. We encourage hiring committees to include a statement of values with hiring calls, clarifying their broad expectations and evaluation criteria around AI use. If it is difficult to include such language due to institutional constraints, faculty in a department may consider doing this as individuals.
